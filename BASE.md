# Oryzhaekund

> The stone violin remembers a song buried beneath the first dragon's shadow.

## A Musician at the Dry Fountain

No one saw the musician arrive. At sunset, he was simply seated beside the dry fountain, holding a violin carved from a single piece of grey stone. Its strings were black, its bridge a pale fragment of horn. The bow rested across his knees like a sleeping insect.

He waited until the square emptied. Then he lifted the bow and drew it across the strings. The fountain remained silent, but water began to fall inside every locked cupboard in the surrounding houses.

The townspeople opened their cupboards and found them dry. They shut the doors and heard the water again. By morning, each household had acquired a cup that nobody recognized. The cups were warm, and their handles pointed toward the fountain regardless of how they were placed.

The musician accepted breakfast without speaking. He left a stone button on the plate. Its four holes formed a constellation that could not be seen from the town.

## The Bow's Unfinished Curve

The bow had been made from the rib of an animal that cast two shadows. One shadow followed the sun. The other followed the sound of bells. Where the shadows crossed, grass grew white and children forgot the names of birds.

An old woman recognized the bow from a dream she had shared with her sister. In that dream, they stood at opposite ends of a bridge and passed a loaf of bread between them without moving their hands. Beneath the bridge, something winged slept with its mouth open.

The woman asked the musician to play a particular note. He placed the violin against his shoulder. The note did not emerge until she had walked away. It followed her through the streets and entered her house ahead of her.

Inside, a cupboard stood open. On its lowest shelf lay the other half of the loaf from her dream, still warm where her sister's fingers had touched it.

## An Instrument Without an Inside

The violin's openings showed no hollow body. Through one, an observer could see a narrow stairway. Through the other, a hillside covered in white grass. The musician kept both openings covered whenever the church bell rang.

A boy once looked through the stairway opening while the bow was moving. He saw an enormous claw resting on a landing. Between its talons lay a small chair, and on the chair sat someone wearing the boy's winter coat.

He told the musician what he had seen. The musician loosened a string, wound it around the stone button, and placed both in the boy's hand. That evening, the boy's coat grew heavier. Its pockets contained grass seeds that shone faintly beneath a blanket.

He planted the seeds beside the fountain. Nothing appeared above the soil, but the sound of roots moving could be heard whenever the square was empty.

## Beneath the Planted Silence

The roots reached a roof buried under the town. Beneath that roof was another square, and in its center a fountain full of dark water. Something enormous lay around it, its wings extending beneath the houses above.

The old woman saw this place in her sleep. The creature's scales were covered in writing. Each line resembled the course of a river, and each river ended at a small stone violin suspended from a hook.

Her sister stood beside the lower fountain, holding an empty cup. She asked whether the music had begun. The old woman tried to answer, but heard only water pouring behind a closed door.

When she woke, the unfamiliar cup in her kitchen had acquired a crack. Through it she could see her sister's hand resting on the other side of the porcelain. The hand moved once, then became still.

## The Inscription Beneath the Chin Rest

On the third evening, the musician turned the violin over. Beneath the place where his jaw had rested were four groups of shallow marks:

*Ory — zhae — kund — vellun.*

The old woman read them and remembered the missing end of her dream. The bridge had never crossed water. It had crossed the folded wing of the creature beneath the town, and the loaf had been passed from one sleep into another.

She brought the bread to the square. The boy brought his coat. The musician laid the bow across both and waited until the church bell struck an hour nobody had counted.

The planted roots became quiet. Every cupboard in the town opened by itself. At the dry fountain, one drop rose from the stone and hung above the basin like a small, unblinking eye.

## The Song After the Player

The musician played until the drop became a doorway. Through it, the old woman saw her sister place the empty cup beside the lower fountain. The winged creature opened one eye. The writing on its scales drifted apart, becoming rivers whose mouths were filled with stars.

The boy's coat lost its weight. The bread crumbled into pale grass seeds. When the last note ended, the musician was gone, and the stone violin lay across the fountain's rim.

No one has moved it. Rain runs over its strings without changing their sound. The cups remain in their cupboards, but their handles now point in different directions.

Some nights, a single note travels through the town. It stops outside a house, waits for someone to dream, and continues on with a second shadow following quietly behind.
